Swedish Dishcloths vs. Paper Towels: 6-Month Cost Test

By The Lighter Footprint Team September 14, 2026 8 min read

Heads up: some links below are affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Here's our full affiliate & advertising disclosure.

The claim shows up everywhere: one $15 pack of Swedish dishcloths replaces 150 rolls of paper towels. It sounds like exactly the kind of too-good statistic that falls apart under scrutiny — so we ran it for six months in a real household and counted everything.

Spoiler: the claim holds up better than we expected. But the details matter, especially the smell part.

The Setup: How We Ran the Test

Household: two adults, one rental apartment, normal cooking habits (home-cooked dinner 5–6 nights a week). Starting inventory on day one: a 10-pack of standard cellulose-cotton Swedish dishcloths ($15) and a 12-pack of store-brand paper towels ($14.40, so $1.20/roll) as backup.

Rules for six months:

  • Dishcloths were the default for wiping counters, cleaning spills, and drying hands.
  • Paper towels were allowed only for jobs the cloths genuinely shouldn't do: bacon grease, pet messes, and anything involving raw chicken.
  • Each time a paper towel roll was finished, the date went on a running list taped inside the cabinet door.
  • Dishcloths went in the regular laundry (hot wash) and were replaced when they fell apart or stopped absorbing.

Before the test, the same household averaged about 4 rolls of paper towels a month — roughly 24 rolls in six months, or about $29.

Month-by-Month: Rolls Avoided and Cloths Retired

  • Month 1: 0.5 rolls used (half a roll for a grease incident). Cloths: all 10 in rotation, zero issues.
  • Month 2: 0.5 rolls. One cloth retired after it went through the wash with a red sock and stained — still functional, just ugly.
  • Month 3: 1 roll (raw chicken month — lots of poultry). First cloth developed a thin spot and was retired. 9 still going.
  • Month 4: 0 rolls. Full dishcloth month. Two more cloths retired with thinning edges.
  • Month 5: 0.5 rolls. The remaining 6 cloths noticeably less absorbent but still usable.
  • Month 6: 0.5 rolls. Two more cloths retired. 4 of the original 10 still in active rotation, slightly frayed.

Total paper towels used in six months: 3 rolls ($3.60) versus ~24 rolls ($28.80) before. That's 21 rolls avoided in six months — on pace for ~42 rolls a year from this household.

The Math: $15 vs. $180, Broken Down

Here's where honesty matters. The famous "150 rolls" figure assumes each cloth replaces 15–17 rolls and that you use all 10 cloths to the end of their lives. Our household pace projects to ~42 rolls/year avoided. At that rate:

  • Paper towels avoided per year: ~42 rolls × $1.20 = ~$50/year
  • Dishcloth cost: $15 for a 10-pack, which lasted well beyond 6 months — projected lifespan ~9–12 months for the pack in our rotation
  • Year-one savings: ~$35, with the pack paying for itself in roughly 3–4 months

The 150-roll claim is best-case and probably assumes heavier paper towel use than ours. But the direction is unambiguous: the pack pays for itself in weeks, not months, and every year after is pure savings. For a household that uses a roll a week (very common), the savings land closer to $60–70/year — and the 150-roll lifetime claim becomes realistic over the pack's full life.

The Smell Question (and How to Fix It)

Let's talk about the thing the glowing reviews skip: yes, they can smell. A damp dishcloth left crumpled on the counter develops a sour, mildewy funk within 48 hours. This happened to us twice in month one.

The fix is boring and it works:

  • Wring them out and drape them flat (over the faucet, a rack, anywhere with airflow) after every use. The smell comes from staying damp, not from the material.
  • Hot wash weekly — ours went in with the regular hot laundry load. No special treatment needed.
  • Boil rescue: a funky cloth simmered in water with a tablespoon of baking soda for 5 minutes comes back to neutral. We did this three times across the six months.
  • Microwave trick: a damp cloth microwaved for 60–90 seconds kills the odor-causing bacteria. Use when you can't run laundry that day.

After we built the drape-flat habit, we had zero smell incidents in months 3–6.

Care Tips: Washing, Drying, When to Compost Them

  • Washing: machine wash hot with regular detergent. They can go in the dishwasher's top rack too — the heat sanitizes them between loads.
  • Drying: air-dry flat or tumble dry low. High heat shortens their life.
  • Rotation: we ran 4–5 in active rotation with the rest clean in a drawer. That cadence kept a fresh one always available.
  • End of life: retired cloths went to the "gross jobs" pile (baseboards, the litter box area, car windows) for another few months. When they're truly shredded, the plain cellulose-cotton ones can go in home compost — snip out any printed ink areas first if you're particular.
  • Don't buy the ones with a plastic layer if composting matters to you — some cheaper versions laminate the cellulose. Check the label.

Verdict: Who Should Buy These (and Who Shouldn't)

Buy them if: you go through more than a roll of paper towels a month, you cook regularly, or you want the single fastest-payback swap in the low-waste world. This is the swap we recommend first to everyone, and the test confirmed why.

Skip them (for now) if: you genuinely use fewer than 6 rolls a year — your payback is fine but the savings are trivial — or if you know you'll never build the rinse-and-drape habit, in which case you'll just own 10 smelly cloths. Be honest with yourself; the $15 isn't worth it as a guilt purchase.

And keep one roll of paper towels around for the grease and raw-chicken jobs. The goal is fewer rolls, not zero rolls with extra stress.

FAQ

How long does one Swedish dishcloth actually last?
In our test, with hot weekly washes, cloths stayed in good shape for 3–4 months of regular use, with some lasting the full 6 months. Expect 200+ uses per cloth with basic care.
Do Swedish dishcloths really replace 15 rolls of paper towels each?
That's the manufacturer's best-case figure. Our measured pace came to roughly 4–5 rolls avoided per cloth per year — which still pays for the pack many times over. Heavier paper-towel users will land closer to the headline claim.
Why do my Swedish dishcloths smell, and how do I fix it?
They smell when left damp and crumpled. Wring out and drape flat after every use, hot-wash weekly, and boil with baking soda or microwave 60–90 seconds to rescue a funky one.
Can you compost Swedish dishcloths?
Plain cellulose-cotton ones, yes — they're home-compostable once shredded. Avoid versions with a plastic laminate layer if composting is your plan.
